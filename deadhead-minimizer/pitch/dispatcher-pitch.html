How it actually works

No new habits to learn. It slots into the same moment a dispatcher already checks the board — right after a load delivers.

01

A load delivers

The truck drops its load in, say, Atlanta. That's the moment that matters most — and the moment most dispatchers are flying blind.

02

The map lights up around it

A live map shows every nearby market color-coded — dark red means plenty of freight and few trucks, grey means the opposite. One glance tells the story.

This is the heat map
03

The system ranks the options

Instead of ten cities to think about, the dispatcher sees a short, ranked list: nearest strong market first, with the deadhead miles and expected rate already worked out.

04

The dispatcher makes the call

Broker's name and number are right there. The dispatcher negotiates the way they always have — just starting from the best option instead of a guess.

Human stays in control

Thinking a week ahead, not just one load

Beyond the next load, the system can plan a full loop — three stops that keep a truck circulating through strong markets all week, and bring it back close to home for the next one.

Chicago → Dallas $2.40/mi
Dallas → Atlanta $2.35/mi
Atlanta → Chicago $2.55/mi
Same truck, same week, no dead miles between stops — and it's back in Chicago ready to run it again.

What the colors mean

The whole system is built around one simple map. No dashboards to learn — just four colors.

Hot zone
Way more freight than trucks. Head here.
Balanced
Healthy market, steady rates.
Soft
More trucks than loads. Worth a look, not a rush.
Dead zone
Plenty of trucks, barely any freight. Avoid sitting here.
